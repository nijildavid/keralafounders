# Open questions: Ireland (agent draft, unreviewed; agent review, not legal advice)

## Judgment calls made by the drafting agent
1. Added one country-specific section, `eea-director`, for the EEA-resident director rule (Companies Act 2014 s.137, with s.138 bond and s.140 certificate). It is the trap non-EEA founders hit first.
2. Chose `next_check_due` 2027-01-15 because Budget 2027 was announced on 6 October 2026 and tax, USC, PRSI and VAT figures may reset on 1 January 2027.
3. Classified Citizens Information (a statutory information body) as `government_portal` and used it for the SURE "up to 41%" figure, which is NOT on Revenue's own SURE page. A reviewer should confirm 41% against Revenue or the Finance Act.
4. Used the Irish Statute Book and Law Reform Commission Revised Acts as `official` sources for company law, because the CRO website blocked automated access (HTTP 403 Cloudflare challenge) all session. Company fees come from S.I. 329/2025, not the CRO fee page.
5. Used the European Commission eInvoicing country page for the e-invoicing position, because no Irish government e-invoicing page could be opened. It is dated 6 March 2026.
6. Wrote "no minimum capital amount was found in the Act" rather than "there is no minimum capital": only s.19 was read, which requires at least one share per subscriber.
7. The whole visa section is held (all claims confidence "hold", as the Standard requires), including figures taken straight from ISD and DETE.
8. Described the Immigrant Investor Programme only as a conflict, not a route, because ISD's FAQ says it closed on 15 February 2023.

## Conflicts between sources
- ISD "Coming to work for more than 90 days" (updated 14 Oct 2025) still describes the Immigrant Investor Programme as open (EUR 1m / EUR 500,000). ISD's own FAQ says it closed to new applications from 15 February 2023. FAQ followed.
- Citizens Information "Starting a business" (edited 26 March 2026) cites the Earned Income Tax Credit "in 2025" at EUR 2,000; Revenue's 2026 table also shows EUR 2,000 for 2026. No conflict in the figure, only in the year.
- DETE's Fees page is headed "received on or after 1 October 2014" and may be stale; it agrees with the Critical Skills page on EUR 1,000, and only the Critical Skills page figure was used.
- The STEP web page refers to "2019 Guidelines and Application form" while the linked files are dated September 2023 and March 2023.
- The 2023 STEP guidelines still describe Ireland's 12.5% corporate tax and 2009-2012 rankings; only the permit terms were used.
